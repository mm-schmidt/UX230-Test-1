Physics and the World Wide Web

In the early 1980s, Tim Berners-Lee was working as an independent contractor at CERN, the European Laboratory for Particle Physics. There, he began exploring a hypertext-based project to facilitate sharing and updating research among researchers. In 1989, he proposed a digital information management system and later that year implemented the first successful Internet communication between an HTTP client and server.

In 1991, Berners-Lee launched the concept at CERN, and two years later released it into the public domain as the World Wide Web.

Whereas Berners-Lee's original concept was intended to be shared only within the world of physics research, the World Wide Web has since been universally adopted, shared, and built upon by billions of people worldwide.
